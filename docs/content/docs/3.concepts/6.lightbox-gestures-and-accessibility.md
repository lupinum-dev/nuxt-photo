The included lightbox is a modal viewer. While open, it owns keyboard input,
moves focus into the viewer, and makes the page behind it inert.

## Opening animation

A FLIP transition measures the thumbnail's first position and the slide's final
position, then animates between them. FLIP means First, Last, Invert, Play.

| Mode   | Behavior                                                            |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `auto` | Uses FLIP when enough of the thumbnail is visible; otherwise fades. |
| `flip` | Always tries to animate from the thumbnail.                         |
| `fade` | Cross-fades without moving from the thumbnail.                      |
| `none` | Opens without a transition.                                         |

`auto` is the default. `autoThreshold` defaults to `0.55`, which means at least
55 percent of the thumbnail must be visible. The visible part must also be at
least 80 pixels wide and high; smaller thumbnails fade. Raise the threshold when movement
from partly hidden thumbnails is distracting.

Closing animates to the thumbnail of the photo you were viewing when it closed.
It fades when that thumbnail is gone or outside the viewport.

## Changing photos

The `navigation` option sets how the lightbox moves from one photo to the next.

| Mode        | Behavior                                                            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `slide`     | Photos sit on a strip. A swipe drags the strip with the finger.     |
| `fade`      | The current photo fades out, then the next photo fades in.          |
| `crossfade` | The next photo fades in over the current one; the room never shows. |

`slide` is the default. In `fade` and `crossfade`, a swipe still works: the
photo follows the finger a little and fades with the distance. A long or fast
swipe changes the photo, and a short one settles back.

## Keyboard controls

| Key          | Action                                                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Escape`     | Close and focus the thumbnail of the photo you were viewing.  |
| `ArrowRight` | Show the next photo, or pan when the current image is zoomed. |
| `ArrowLeft`  | Show the previous photo, or pan when zoomed.                  |
| `Home`       | Show the first photo.                                         |
| `End`        | Show the last photo.                                          |
| `z`          | Zoom to the photo's real pixels, or back to fit.              |
| `Tab`        | Move through the lightbox controls, and show hidden controls. |

Right-to-left direction reverses directional navigation. Text fields, text
areas, selects, and editable content keep their keyboard input. Other controls
receive the event before the lightbox. Call `event.preventDefault()` from a
custom control when it must suppress a built-in shortcut such as Escape, an
arrow key, Home, End, or `z`.

## Pointer gestures

A click or tap beside the photo closes the lightbox.

| Input                | On the photo                     | While zoomed       |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Mouse click          | Zoom to the photo's real pixels. | Zoom back to fit.  |
| Touch tap            | Show or hide the controls.       | Show or hide them. |
| Touch double tap     | Zoom to the tapped point.        | Zoom back to fit.  |
| Drag                 | Show the previous or next photo. | Move the photo.    |
| Vertical touch drag  | Close the lightbox.              | Move the photo.    |
| Pinch or mouse wheel | Change the zoom where supported. | Change the zoom.   |

"Real pixels" means one photo pixel per screen pixel, based on the photo's
`width`. Zoom stops at four times the fitted size, or at `minZoom` when that is
larger. `minZoom` sets the lowest click zoom, which keeps zoom useful for photos that are already near full size.
The default is `1.5`. A zoomed photo stops at its own edge, so it never shows
empty space inside the screen.

Hidden controls leave the `Tab` order. A mouse movement or the `Tab` key shows
them again.

## Reduced motion

Nuxt Photo observes `prefers-reduced-motion`. It replaces movement-heavy
transitions with a short fade and reacts when the preference changes. Custom
lightbox content must provide its own reduced-motion treatment.

Use native buttons for custom controls and retain visible focus styles. The
[primitives guide](/docs/guides/build-a-lightbox-from-primitives) shows the
lower-level structure.
